# 06. Content Strategy

Content is how [Company] earns the right to be the #1 brand in player intelligence before the market is fully formed. The goal is not reach for its own sake. The goal is to own the conversation about padel improvement, skill measurement, and sports AI.

---

## Content Mission

**Help padel and pickleball players understand their game better than anyone else is helping them right now.**

If our content is the best educational, analytical, and entertaining resource for club-level padel players, we earn trust and attention before we ask for anything.

---

## The 4 Content Pillars

Every piece of [Company] content maps to one of these pillars. Each pillar serves a specific purpose in the player journey.

---

### Pillar 1: Know Your Game (Education + Player Rating)

**Purpose:** Establish [Company] as the definitive authority on padel skill development and player intelligence.

**Topics:**
- What makes padel skill measurable (breaking down TECH, TACT, PHY)
- How the Player Rating is calculated (accessible version, not technical docs)
- What separates a 600-rated player from an 800-rated player
- Common mistakes at each skill level
- How to read your own match stats
- The role of net control, overpass suppression, and first-phase initiative

**Formats:** Long-form articles. Short-form breakdowns. Infographic explainers. Player Rating level guides.

**Tone:** Expert friend. Specific, accessible, never condescending.

**Content example:** "What your net control percentage actually means, and why most club players have it completely wrong."

---

### Pillar 2: Player Intelligence Reports (Data + Stories)

**Purpose:** Show the product in action. Make data feel exciting, not intimidating.

**Topics:**
- Post-tournament breakdowns (real match data, anonymised or permissioned)
- "A day in the data" stories: one player's game from raw footage to Player Rating
- Stats that surprise: counterintuitive findings from real matches
- Player Rating leaderboards and milestone moments (first 700-rated player at a court, etc.)
- Trend analysis: how does padel skill differ by market?

**Formats:** Data stories. Visual posts. Short-form video breakdowns. Email deep dives.

**Tone:** Data journalist meets sports analyst. Specific, curious, the data is the hero.

**Content example:** "We analysed 200 matches. Here's what separates 3.0 players from 4.0 players."

---

### Pillar 3: Sport and Community (Celebration + Connection)

**Purpose:** Build emotional connection. Remind players why they love this sport.

**Topics:**
- Match highlights (user-generated and [Company] produced)
- Player stories and journeys
- Court spotlights from partner locations
- Sport news and commentary (padel and pickleball)
- Tournament coverage
- Milestones: first Player Rating, biggest improvement, highest-rated match

**Formats:** Short-form video. Photo carousels. Player spotlights. Community posts.

**Tone:** Warm, celebratory, community-first. The sport is the hero.

**Content example:** "She picked up padel six months ago. This week she hit her first Player Rating 500. This is her game breakdown."

---

### Pillar 4: The Science of Sport AI (Credibility + B2B)

**Purpose:** Establish [Company] as a serious AI company, not just a sports app. Relevant for investors, partners, and the sports tech industry.

**Topics:**
- How DreamLens and our model stack works (accessible)
- The challenge of building AI for padel (why 95% accuracy matters)
- Our data moat explained
- The state of sports AI globally
- Vision Language Models applied to racquet sports
- Industry commentary and thought leadership from the leadership team

**Formats:** LinkedIn thought leadership. Long-form industry articles. Conference presentations. Podcast appearances.

**Tone:** Authoritative, credible, industry-facing. Slightly more formal than consumer content.

**Content example:** "Why raw AI gets padel wrong 95% of the time, and what we did about it."

## Channel Playbook

### Instagram and TikTok

**Primary use:** Brand awareness, player community, sport celebration, Player Rating storytelling.

**Content mix:**
- 40% Player Intelligence Reports (data posts, stats, Player Rating reveals)
- 30% Sport and Community (highlights, player stories, court moments)
- 20% Know Your Game (quick tips, short breakdowns)
- 10% Brand moments (product launches, milestones, team)

**Posting cadence:** 4 to 5 times per week. Stories daily when there is active match coverage.

**Key formats:**
- Player Rating reveal reels (player scores their first Player Rating or breaks a milestone)
- Match highlights with data overlays
- "Your game tells you this" stat breakdowns
- Tournament coverage carousels
- "We analysed 100 matches and found..." data posts

**Rules:**
- Always show real courts and real players. No stock.
- Data overlays are the signature. Use them.
- Captions lead with the player, not the product.
- Hashtag strategy: own #companyrating and #playerintelligence; use sport-specific tags (#padel, #pickleball, #padelworld) for reach.

---

### LinkedIn

**Primary use:** Industry credibility, B2B pipeline, investor visibility, talent attraction.

**Content mix:**
- 50% The Science of Sport AI (thought leadership)
- 30% Traction and milestones (company news, expansion, data)
- 20% Player Intelligence Reports (data stories with professional angle)

**Posting cadence:** 3 times per week.

**Key formats:**
- CEO and leadership posts on sports AI and the player intelligence category
- Traction announcements (new markets, court partnerships, tournament coverage)
- Data posts framed for professional audiences
- Industry commentary on padel and pickleball market growth

**Rules:**
- Personal posts from leadership outperform company page posts. Both matter.
- Lead with an opinion or a number, not a company update.
- Long-form LinkedIn articles for pillar 4 topics.

---

### Email (Player)

**Primary use:** Retention, match analysis delivery, personalised coaching.

**Types:**
- Post-match analysis email (transactional: delivered after every covered match)
- Weekly Player Rating report (for active users: how has your rating moved this week)
- Improvement pathway emails (coaching series based on player's specific Player Rating profile)
- Re-engagement (for players who haven't played in 3+ weeks)

**Post-match analysis template:**
1. Subject: "Your Player Rating after [Date] match: [Score]"
2. Open: one sentence on the match result and overall Player Rating change
3. Three-pillar breakdown (TECH, TACT, PHY) with the biggest finding per pillar
4. One focused improvement: the single highest-leverage thing to work on this week
5. CTA: view full match breakdown in app

**Rules:**
- Every email has exactly one primary CTA.
- Lead with the player's data, not the product.
- Subject lines always include the Player Rating score or a specific number.
- No email over 250 words for post-match. 150 is better.

---

### App (In-Product Content)

**Primary use:** Onboarding, engagement, habit formation, upsell.

**Key moments:**
- First Player Rating reveal: This is a brand moment. Make it feel like an achievement, not a number loading.
- Post-match notification: One line. "Your Player Rating updated: 547 (+12)." That's the hook. Click to see more.
- Empty state (no matches yet): Don't say "no data." Say "Your Player Rating starts with your first match. We're ready when you are."
- Milestone moments: First match covered. First Player Rating 500. First improvement streak. Celebrate these explicitly.
- Weekly summary: Delivered Sunday. One key trend from the past week's matches.

**Rules:**
- Every empty state has a clear, encouraging next action.
- Loading states should have a micro-copy moment, not a spinner.
- Error messages explain what happened and what to do. Never just say "error."

## Content Calendar Principles

1. **Match weeks are publishing weeks.** When we're covering a tournament, content output doubles. Plan accordingly.
2. **One hero piece per month.** A long-form data story, a major breakdown, or a significant player feature. This is the content we're known for.
3. **Player Rating milestones trigger content.** First player to hit 800 Player Rating, 100th match at a court, 1000th user: these are stories.
4. **Market expansion triggers brand awareness push.** When we enter a new geography, the first 4 weeks are aggressive brand awareness content in that market.
5. **Never post just to fill the calendar.** If there's nothing worth saying, say nothing. Volume without quality erodes the brand.
