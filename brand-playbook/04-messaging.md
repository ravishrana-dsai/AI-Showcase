# 04. Messaging Framework

This is what [Company] says, to whom, and when. The core narratives are the engine. The taglines and pitches are the expression of those narratives in different contexts and formats.

---

## Primary Tagline

**"Know Your Game."**

This is the [Company] line. Simple, three words, owned by nobody else, and carries multiple meanings simultaneously:

- Know your skill level (the Player Rating gives you the number)
- Know your strengths and weaknesses (game analysis)
- Know padel or pickleball as a sport, more deeply than before
- Know what to work on next (coaching paths)

It positions without explaining. It invites curiosity. It works on an app screen, a billboard, a jersey, and an investor deck cover.

**Tagline usage:** Use on primary brand touchpoints. Do not dilute by changing it for campaigns. The campaign can have its own line; the brand has this one.

## Core Brand Narratives

These are the five stories [Company] tells. Every major piece of communication anchors to one of these.

---

### Narrative 1: The Rating Problem

**The insight:** Every padel player has a "level" but nobody actually knows theirs. Self-assessed ratings are optimistic at best, useless at worst. Tournament rankings reflect a handful of results. Coach assessments vary wildly by coach.

**The claim:** [Company] is the first scientifically derived, video-powered skill rating in amateur padel. The Player Rating is objective, consistent, and built on real match data, not opinion.

**The proof:** 80% SkillScore accuracy versus less than 5% for raw AI tools. 5M+ data points per match. 95%+ consistency across assessments.

**Use when:** Introducing the product to new users. App onboarding. Ads targeting competitive club players. Investor narrative.

**Core line:** "Self-assessed ratings are useless. Your Player Rating is not."

---

### Narrative 2: The Data Moat

**The insight:** Building AI for sport sounds easy. It is not. Generic AI tools applied to padel video get the wrong answer more than 95% of the time. Getting to 80% accuracy took 18 months, 1,000+ matches of training, and $200M+ worth of annotated data.

**The claim:** The Player Rating cannot be replicated with an API call. Our data moat is the real barrier to entry, and it compounds every time a new match is processed.

**The proof:** 16x accuracy advantage over generic AI. 20,000+ hours of video. 3M+ annotated images. 20+ classical models combined with 50+ fine-tuned VLMs.

**Use when:** Investor pitches. Partnership discussions. Press and media. Team and hiring.

**Core line:** "What took us 18 months and 1,000 matches to build cannot be replicated with an API call."

---

### Narrative 3: The Strava Moment

**The insight:** The sports analytics market built tools for courts, coaches, and federations. Nobody built for the player. The consumer layer is wide open. This is the same gap Strava exploited when every other fitness brand was B2B.

**The claim:** [Company] is building the consumer intelligence layer in racquet sports. D2C-first, player-obsessed, with the data and product depth to build a category-defining brand.

**The proof:** [Company] built the dominant D2C sports product in India (260M users). We are applying the same playbook to player intelligence globally.

**Use when:** Strategic investor conversations. Conference talks. Industry press. Partnerships with leagues and tours.

**Core line:** "Everyone built for the court. We built for the player."

---

### Narrative 4: The Market Timing

**The insight:** Padel and pickleball are not niche sports. They are the fastest growing racquet sports globally, with 50M combined players today and 110M by 2030. The amateur player base is young (70% of pickleball players are 18 to 44), tech-native, and data-hungry.

**The claim:** This is the right sport, at the right moment, for the right product. Players who pick up padel today will form habits and loyalties in the next 12 to 24 months. [Company] needs to be the default intelligence layer when that happens.

**The proof:** 32M padel players, 22.7M pickleball players. Court counts doubling by 2030. $10-20B annual market spend. 70-80 new sign-ups from every single tournament we cover.

**Use when:** General brand storytelling. Partnership pitches. Social content introducing the sport. Investor decks section on market.

**Core line:** "50 million players. No one knew how good any of them actually were. Until now."

---

### Narrative 5: The Flywheel

**The insight:** [Company] is already working. 1,200+ matches, 500+ users, 9 courts, 7-9 tournaments, three months since MVP. Invited to the World Padel Summit. Inbound from South America, UK, Spain, Middle East. This is traction, not a pitch.

**The claim:** The flywheel is spinning. More matches mean more data, better models, higher Player Rating accuracy, more compelling product, more users, more matches. The lead extends with every match played.

**The proof:** 500+ registered users (140 repeat), 1,200+ matches covered, 1,500+ user requests in under 3 months. Strong inbound from multiple geographies.

**Use when:** Fundraising. Hiring. Court operator pitches. Press and media.

**Core line:** "The flywheel is already spinning. The gap is widening."

## Messages to Avoid

These sound reasonable but are off-brand. Do not use them.

- "We democratise data." (Everyone says this. It means nothing.)
- "We're using AI to revolutionise padel." (Vague and overused.)
- "Our platform is easy to use." (Show it, don't say it.)
- "We help players reach their potential." (Too soft. What do we actually do?)
- "We're passionate about sports." (Every sports brand says this.)
- "We're disrupting the industry." (The Player Rating data is the disruption. Show the numbers.)
