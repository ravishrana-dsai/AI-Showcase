## Design Context

### Users
Small content and marketing team at [Company] (2-5 people). Internal tool used daily to generate, review, and publish brand content across LinkedIn, blogs, Reddit, and newsletters. They are smart, fast-moving, and expect the tool to feel as premium as the product they're marketing — a scientific sports intelligence platform.

### Brand Personality
Confident, premium, bold. [Company] is not a generic AI startup. It is the Strava of racquet sports — data-driven, authoritative, built on a $200M+ data moat. The tool should feel like a professional command center, not a side project. Words: **authoritative**, **precise**, **kinetic**.

### Aesthetic Direction
Sports intelligence editorial. Think Bloomberg Terminal seriousness crossed with the visual energy of sports data broadcasting. Dark backgrounds with warm authority (not cold tech startup), a strong typographic hierarchy, and a palette that signals performance and precision.

Anti-references: generic AI startup dashboards (cyan on dark, lime glow on black, Geist font, identical card grids, hero metrics). This should not look like it was generated in five minutes.

Palette: Full rethink. The current zero-chroma neutral grays and harsh lime-on-black combination has no warmth or authority. Move toward deep warm charcoal backgrounds, tinted neutrals, and an accent that signals performance — consider a bold amber-gold or a rich electric orange as the primary accent rather than the AI-stereotyped lime green.

Theme: Dark only.

### Design Principles
1. **Authority over decoration** — every visual decision should reinforce credibility, not add visual noise.
2. **Information density with breathing room** — this is a command center; pack meaningful data but give the eye places to rest.
3. **Kinetic restraint** — the product is about motion and sport, but the dashboard communicates through precision, not animation. Motion serves state changes only.
4. **No card addiction** — not everything needs a container. Use space, type, and color to group; reserve card borders for true containment.
5. **Typography leads** — strong type hierarchy communicates status and hierarchy before color does. Headlines should be unmistakably different from body copy.
