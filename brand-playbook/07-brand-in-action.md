# 07. Brand in Action

This section is the practical layer of the Brand Bible. Use it to evaluate copy before publishing, rewrite off-brand content, and produce new material quickly. If you're unsure whether something sounds like [Company], this is where you find out.

---

## Before and After Rewrites

These show the difference between generic copy and [Company] copy. Study the pattern, not just the examples.

---

### Example 1: App Onboarding Headline

**Before (generic):**
"Welcome to [Company]. Get started with your personalized sports analytics experience."

**After ([Company]):**
"Your first match is your baseline. Let's find out where your game actually stands."

**What changed:** Removed corporate welcome. Removed "personalized" (hollow). Introduced the concept of a baseline, which is central to Player Rating. Made the player feel like this is about discovery, not setup.

---

### Example 2: Post-Match Notification

**Before (generic):**
"Your match analysis is ready! Open the app to see your performance insights."

**After ([Company]):**
"Player Rating updated: 547 (+12). Your net game drove it. See the full breakdown."

**What changed:** Led with the number. Named the specific thing that drove the improvement (net game, not vague "performance"). Cut the exclamation point marketing energy. Made it a fact, not a sales pitch.

---

### Example 3: Instagram Caption

**Before (generic):**
"Every match is a chance to improve. Track your progress with [Company]'s AI-powered analytics. Link in bio to get started! #padel #padellife #sports"

**After ([Company]):**
"He spent 42% of that rally at the net. The average club player? 27%. That's not luck. That's a decision he makes every point. Player Rating: 634."

**What changed:** Removed the generic call to action. Replaced with a specific stat. Gave context (versus average). Made it about a real player making real decisions. The product is implied, not sold.

---

### Example 4: Investor Deck Opening Line

**Before (generic):**
"[Company] is an AI-powered sports analytics platform leveraging cutting-edge machine learning to provide actionable insights for padel players."

**After ([Company]):**
"50 million padel and pickleball players. No one had any idea how good any of them actually were. We fixed that."

**What changed:** Removed every piece of jargon. Opened with the market size. Named the problem clearly. The last sentence is a claim you can disagree with (someone could say "that's not true"), which means it's a real claim.

---

### Example 5: Error State (No Match Data Yet)

**Before (generic):**
"No data available. Please record a match to see your analytics."

**After ([Company]):**
"Nothing here yet. Play your first match at any [Company] court and your Player Rating appears within hours."

**What changed:** "Nothing here yet" is human, not robotic. Replaced "record a match" (technical, passive) with "play your first match" (the player is the actor). Named the Player Rating specifically. Gave a time expectation ("within hours").

---

### Example 6: LinkedIn Post (Leadership / Thought Leadership)

**Before (generic):**
"Excited to announce that [Company] is expanding to new markets! We're leveraging our innovative AI technology to empower players worldwide. Stay tuned for more updates!"

**After ([Company]):**
"Three months since MVP launch. 1,200 matches processed. 500 users. 9 courts. Inbound from Spain, UK, South America, and the Middle East. We haven't done a single outbound sales call. The Player Rating is doing the selling."

**What changed:** Removed announcement-speak. Led with real numbers. Ended with a surprising, specific claim that tells a story on its own. The tone is confident and factual, not excited and vague.

---

### Example 7: Court Partnership Pitch Line

**Before (generic):**
"Partner with [Company] to provide your players with an enhanced experience using our state-of-the-art analytics platform."

**After ([Company]):**
"Give your players a Player Rating. Our courts are seeing 70 to 80 new sign-ups per tournament covered. Players come back because they have a score to improve."

**What changed:** Led with the outcome the court operator cares about (player retention). Backed it with a real number (70-80 sign-ups per tournament). Cut all the jargon.

## Copy Templates

### Template 1: Player Rating Milestone Post (Social)

```
[Player name / "One of our players"] just hit Player Rating [score].

[One sentence on what they do well, with a specific stat.]

[One sentence on what this score means in context: "That puts them in the top X% of players at their court."]

[One sentence on what got them there, something they changed or improved.]

Player Rating [score]. Earned.
```

**Example:**
"Rodrigo just hit Player Rating 712. His net control time is 54%. The average at his club? 31%. He's been working his approach shot for six weeks. Player Rating 712. Earned."

---

### Template 2: Post-Match Email Subject Line

Format: `Your Player Rating after [Day]: [Score] ([+/- change])`

Examples:
- "Your Player Rating after Tuesday: 547 (+12)"
- "Your Player Rating after Sunday's tournament match: 623 (–8)"
- "You played three matches this week. Your Player Rating: 589 (+21)"

---

### Template 3: Stat Callout (Data Post)

```
[Surprising or counterintuitive stat.]

[Context: what the average is, or why this matters.]

[What it means for how you play.]
```

**Example:**
"74% of points at club level are won before the overhead.

Most players practice their overhead. They should be practicing the first four shots.

This is what your First-Phase Initiative Index measures."

---

### Template 4: App Empty State

```
[Acknowledge the empty state honestly, one short sentence.]
[Tell them exactly what to do and what happens when they do it.]
```

**Examples:**
- "No matches yet. Play at any [Company] court and your Player Rating appears within hours."
- "Your improvement trend starts here. After three covered matches, we show you the direction your game is heading."
- "No coaching insights yet. Your first Player Rating unlocks a personalised improvement plan."

---

### Template 5: Short Bio / About Us (100 words)

"[Company] is the Player Intelligence platform for padel and pickleball. We turn raw match footage into the [Company] Rating: a scientific, video-derived skill score that tells you exactly where your game stands. Not a self-assessment. Not a guess. A number built from 5 million data points per match.

We are part of [Company], the company behind [Company]. Our models are 16 times more accurate than generic AI tools. We have processed 1,200+ matches since launching in December 2025.

Know your game."

## Master Brand Audit Checklist

Run every piece of [Company] content through this before publishing.

### Voice Check
- [ ] Does it sound like a person who knows the sport, not a content generator?
- [ ] Is it specific enough that a competitor could NOT have written the same thing?
- [ ] Does it say something a reasonable person could disagree with?
- [ ] Does it pass the read-aloud test? (Read it out loud. Does it sound natural?)
- [ ] Are all em-dashes removed and replaced with appropriate alternatives?

### Messaging Check
- [ ] Does it connect to one of the five core narratives?
- [ ] Is the word "Player Intelligence" or "Player Rating" used where relevant?
- [ ] Are all claims backed by a proof point (stat, data point, or specific example)?
- [ ] Is "Know Your Game." or a sub-tagline present where appropriate?
- [ ] Is the player the subject of the sentence, not the product?

### Vocabulary Check
- [ ] None of the banned words used (leverage, synergy, seamless, robust, empower, journey, etc.)?
- [ ] Brand names correct? "[Company]" (not [Company]), "Player Rating" (not "our rating"), "[Company] AI" for the venture?
- [ ] Technical metrics explained on first use if consumer-facing?

### Visual Check (for designed assets)
- [ ] Uses only the approved color palette?
- [ ] Data visualizations follow brand standards (metric colors, no Excel aesthetic)?
- [ ] Photography is real, not stock?
- [ ] Player Rating score displayed in the correct typeface and weight?
- [ ] Logo usage follows lockup rules?

### Audience Check
- [ ] Is the intended audience clear?
- [ ] Is the tone calibrated to that audience (consumer vs. B2B vs. investor)?
- [ ] Does the CTA match what this audience should do next?

### The One-Rule Check
- [ ] Does this make a player think: "This brand actually understands my game"?

If any box is unchecked, revise before publishing.

## Phrases That Are Always Off-Brand

Print this. Put it next to wherever copy gets written.

| Never say | Because |
|---|---|
| "We're excited to announce..." | Every brand says this. It is meaningless. |
| "...leveraging AI to..." | Jargon. Say what the AI actually does. |
| "Seamless experience" | Everyone claims this. Prove it, don't say it. |
| "Empower players" | Too soft. What do we actually give them? |
| "Best-in-class" | Prove the claim instead: "16x more accurate." |
| "Your journey" | Players don't think of their padel as a journey. They think of their game. |
| "Unlock your potential" | Generic fitness brand language. Not ours. |
| "State-of-the-art" | Describe the technology specifically instead. |
| "Passionate about sport" | Everyone says this. Say what you actually do. |
| "We're disrupting..." | Show the disruption. Don't label it. |
