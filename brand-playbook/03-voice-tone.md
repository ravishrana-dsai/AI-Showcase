# 03. Voice and Tone

Voice is who [Company] is. Tone is how we adapt that personality to the context. The voice never changes. The tone flexes.

---

## The [Company] Voice

**The Expert Friend.**

Think of someone who played competitive padel at university, now works in sports tech, knows the biomechanics of a bandeja and can explain it without making you feel dumb, gets genuinely lit up when you tell them you won a match, and can always pull out a stat to back a point.

That is the voice. Not a brand voice. A person's voice. Warm, specific, confident, earned.

| Dimension | Score | Meaning |
|---|---|---|

## This-But-Not-That

| We Are | We Are Not |
|---|---|
| Confident ("Our models are 16x more accurate") | Arrogant ("Others are just doing it wrong") |
| Precise ("Your Net Control Time is 34%, top players are at 52%") | Vague ("You could improve your net game") |
| Warm ("You played a great third game") | Sycophantic ("Amazing performance!!!") |
| Bold ("Self-assessed ratings are useless") | Reckless ("Our competitors are liars") |
| Passionate ("This sport is exploding, and you're part of it") | Hype ("The biggest thing to hit sports EVER!") |
| Expert ("Your First-Phase Initiative Index dropped under pressure") | Jargon-dumping without context |
| Encouraging ("Here's exactly what to work on") | Preachy ("You need to practice more") |
| Playful occasionally | Trying to be funny all the time |

## Vocabulary

### Words We Own and Use

These are [Company] words. Use them with consistency.

- **Player Intelligence** — our category. Use it to describe what we do at the highest level.
- **Player Rating / [Company] Rating** — always capitalised. The core product. The number that matters.
- **Scientific skill score** — how we describe Player Rating. Not "algorithm-based rating" or "AI score."
- **Match footage** — not "video content" or "recordings." Footage is sport-specific and precise.
- **Game analysis** — not "analytics" in consumer contexts. Analysis is human. Analytics is software.
- **Performance intelligence** — for B2B and investor contexts.
- **Precision** — our AI characteristic. Not "smart" or "powerful."
- **Objective** — what makes Player Rating different from every other rating. Use it deliberately.
- **Club player** — our primary customer. Not "amateur" unless in a specific context.
- **Improve** — active and forward-looking. Preferred over "get better" (vague) or "develop" (corporate).
- **Court** — not "venue" or "facility."
- **Match** — not "game" for padel (game has a specific scoring meaning).
- **Rally** — specific padel vocabulary. Use it.

### Words We Avoid

| Avoid | Use Instead |
|---|---|
| Leverage | Use |
| Solution | Product / platform |
| Synergy | (never) |
| Best-in-class | 16x more accurate than generic AI |
| Cutting-edge | Specific claim instead |
| Innovative | Show the innovation, don't label it |
| Empower | Give / help / equip |
| Journey | Improvement / progress / path |
| Seamless | (never) |
| Robust | Reliable / proven |
| Utilize | Use |
| Actionable insights | Specific: "tells you exactly which shots to fix" |
| Space | Sport / category / market |
| Algorithm | Model / AI system (unless technical audience) |

### Jargon Policy

**Consumer-facing content (app, social, ads):** Zero unexplained jargon. If a metric name is used, explain it in the same breath. "Your Net Control Time (the percentage of the rally you spend near the net) is 34%."

**Coaching and analysis content:** Technical terms are expected and welcome. Players reading in-depth breakdowns want the vocabulary. Define once, use freely.

**B2B and investor content:** Full technical vocabulary. Vision Language Models, inference cost, SkillScore accuracy, dataset replacement cost. These audiences expect precision and are put off by dumbing down.

## Tone by Channel

The voice stays the same. These are tone adjustments, not personality changes.

| Channel | Tone Adjustment | Key Principle |
|---|---|---|
| **App (Player Rating + Analysis screens)** | Precise, encouraging, personal | The player is looking at their own data. Be specific and be on their side. |
| **App (empty states / onboarding)** | Warm, inviting, slightly playful | First impression. Make it feel like a welcome, not a tutorial. |
| **Push notifications** | Short, direct, slightly exciting | You have 3 seconds. Make it count. One idea only. |
| **Email (transactional)** | Warm and clear | You played a match, here's what happened. No fluff, just value. |
| **Email (marketing / retention)** | Conversational, story-driven | Read like a letter from someone who follows your progress. |
| **Instagram / TikTok** | Most casual, sport-first, visual | The sport and the player are the hero. Data is the supporting character. |
| **LinkedIn** | Credible, confident, industry-facing | For court operators, investors, sport industry. Tone up formality slightly. |
| **Press / PR** | Authoritative, factual | Let the numbers lead. Quotes should be bold and specific. |
| **Investor decks** | Precise, confident, evidence-backed | Every claim needs a proof point. Narrative must be watertight. |
| **Tournament comms** | Energetic, competitive, community | It's match day. This is the moment. Reflect that energy. |

## Writing Principles

### 1. Lead with the player, not the product.
Wrong: "[Company]'s AI analyses your match footage to generate a Player Rating."
Right: "You played 23 minutes. Here's what your game is telling you."

### 2. Be specific. Specificity is credibility.
Wrong: "We analyse your shots."
Right: "We track every shot, rally, and movement decision across 5M+ data points per match."

### 3. Say something a reasonable person could disagree with.
This is the test for whether copy is human. "Padel ratings based on self-assessment are meaningless" is a real opinion. "We provide insights" is not.

### 4. Short sentences carry further.
[Company] copy is not long paragraphs. It moves. One idea per sentence. Scan-friendly. Punchy.

### 5. No em-dashes. No filler.
Never use em-dashes. Rewrite the sentence. Cut anything that doesn't add information or energy.

### 6. Read it aloud before publishing.
If it sounds like a press release or a software manual, rewrite it. [Company] copy sounds like a person who knows what they're talking about.

---

## The "Un-AI" Test

Before publishing any piece of copy, ask: could a generic AI tool have written this?

If yes, it is not [Company] copy. It needs:
- A specific stat or data point
- A point of view someone could argue with
- A reference to the actual experience of playing padel or pickleball
- A sentence that sounds like a person, not a content generator

[Company] copy never sounds like it was generated by a tool. It sounds like it was written by someone who cares about the sport, knows the science, and respects the reader's intelligence.
